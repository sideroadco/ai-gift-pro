Gifts for teenage boys (13 to 17) they will actually use

Published November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer

The best gifts for teenage boys make something he already does better: a JBL Clip 5 speaker for the boy who plays music everywhere, a HyperX Cloud III Wireless headset for online gaming, AirTags for the one who loses everything, or a Philips Norelco OneBlade for a first shave. What do teenage boys actually want as a gift?

Most teenage boys want one of three things: something that makes the thing they already do better, something they can use with their friends, or something that makes them look a little more grown up. That is the whole list. Very few of them want a surprise, and almost none of them want a gift with a lesson attached.

The gift guides that rank for this topic tend to stack fifty products in a row and leave you to guess which one suits your kid. The more useful question is what he does with his free hours. A 14 year old who skates every afternoon and a 17 year old who just got a part time job need very different gifts, even though both are "teen boys." Start with his week, not with a list.

Gifts he will use with his friends

Teenage boys spend a surprising share of their time in groups, and the gifts that last are often the ones that give the group something to do. Several of the teen gift lists we read, including the picks collected by Grown and Flown from parents of teenagers, lean heavily on gear that gets passed around.

  1. A clip on speaker. The JBL Clip 5 hooks onto a backpack strap or a bike, is built to survive water, and is loud enough for a driveway basketball game. It is the kind of thing that ends up at every gathering.
  2. A yard game that is actually competitive. The Spikeball Standard set is fast, easy to learn and hard to master, which is exactly what a group of 15 year olds wants. It packs into a bag and works on grass, sand or a gym floor.
  3. A headset for the nightly group call. If his friends meet up online, a good wireless headset like the HyperX Cloud III Wireless is a real upgrade over the earbuds he uses now. The microphone matters as much as the sound, because the point is talking to people. Our guide to gifts for gamers covers how to check which console or PC he uses before you buy.

Group gifts also solve a quiet problem: they give a teen a reason to invite people over. That is worth more to some boys than anything they could use alone.

Practical gifts that do not feel practical

Parents usually want to buy something useful, and teens usually resist anything that looks like a chore. The trick is to pick useful things he already wishes he had.

A phone that dies at 3 p.m. is a constant source of friction in a teen house. A slim Anker Nano power bank fits in a pocket and keeps him reachable, which helps you as much as him. CNN Underscored puts one in its teen boy gift guide for exactly that reason.

The Apple AirTag 4 pack is the other quiet winner. Put one on his keys, one in his wallet and one in his sports bag, and he will thank you the first time he finds his stuff in a locker room. It only works with an iPhone, so check what phone he carries first.

A good water bottle sounds boring until you watch how many plastic ones a teenage athlete goes through. The Hydro Flask 32 oz Wide Mouth keeps drinks cold through a long practice and survives being dropped on a gym floor. Pick his team color and it stops being a lecture about hydration and becomes his bottle.

Gifts for the boy who cares about how he looks

Somewhere around 14, a lot of boys start caring about sneakers, hair and how they smell. Gifts here work best when they help him take care of what he already likes rather than telling him what to wear.

If he has a pair of sneakers he babies, the Jason Markk Essential kit is a small gift that says you noticed. It cleans leather, suede and mesh, and it pairs well with a gift card to the sneaker store he likes.

For a first shave, the Philips Norelco OneBlade is forgiving. It trims and shaves without the nicks that put teens off a manual razor, and it works for patchy early facial hair that is not ready for a full routine.

Skin is a sensitive subject, so keep it simple. A box of Starface Hydro-Stars in a stocking is useful and slightly funny, which is the right tone. Avoid giving him a ten step skin care set. PureWow's list, built from teens and their parents, makes the same point: simple beats complicated for this age.

For the athlete, the reader and the builder

Some boys are easy to read because they have one big thing. Lean all the way into it.

The athlete. The Theragun Mini is a splurge, but for a teen with two practices a day it gets used every evening. Smaller options include good performance socks or a duffel big enough for all his gear.

The reader. If he reads at night, a Kindle Paperwhite lets him carry every series he is working through and read in the dark without a lamp. Add a book by an author he already likes, not one you think he should read.

The builder or tinkerer. Older Lego sets, a beginner guitar bundle, or a small drone all reward the boy who likes to take things apart. Choose the version that matches his skill now, because a gift that is too advanced turns into a source of frustration.

The photographer. The Fujifilm Instax Mini Evo prints photos instantly and connects to a phone. Teens like having physical photos from a trip or a game, and it is a gift friends will want to borrow.

How much should you spend on a teenage boy?

For a son, most families land somewhere between $50 and $200 for the main gift, with a few smaller items around it. For a nephew, grandson or family friend, $25 to $50 is plenty, and a well chosen small gift beats an expensive guess. Our guide to how much to spend on a gift goes through budgets by relationship.

Two pieces of budget advice matter more than the number itself. First, cheaper versions of things he will lose are fine. A parent quoted in one guide said a cheap wallet is the right wallet for a teen, because losing it is not a tragedy. Second, if you are tempted to spend big, check that the gift fits him today and not the version of him you expect in two years.

What should you avoid buying a teenage boy?

A few gift types reliably miss with this age group, usually because they carry a message he can hear.

  • Anything that sounds like homework. Study planners, vocabulary games and self improvement books read as instructions.
  • Clothes you picked for him. Unless you know the exact brand and size he wears, give the store gift card instead. Teens are very particular about fit.
  • Gifts that suit a younger kid. A toy or theme he loved at 11 can feel embarrassing at 14. When in doubt, aim older.
  • Gear with strings attached. A gift that comes with new rules about screen time or chores stops feeling like a gift.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good gift for a 13 year old boy?

A good gift for a 13 year old boy supports something he already does a lot, like gaming, sports or music. A clip on Bluetooth speaker, a yard game such as Spikeball, or a wireless headset for online games are safe choices that do not feel too young or too grown up.

What do 16 and 17 year old boys want for Christmas?

Older teens tend to want gear that fits a more independent life: AirTags for keys and wallet, a power bank, a good backpack or duffel, earbuds, a first electric shaver, or a gift card for the stores and games they already use. Many also appreciate money toward a bigger goal like a car or a trip.

Is a gift card a lazy gift for a teenager?

Not for a teenager. Teens are particular about clothes, sneakers and games, and a gift card to the exact store or platform he uses lets him choose. Pair it with a small physical item, such as a sneaker cleaning kit with a sneaker store card, so there is still something to open.

How do I find out what a teen boy wants without asking him?

Look at what he uses until it breaks: dead earbuds, a cracked water bottle, a backpack with a broken zipper. Ask a sibling, cousin or friend his age what he has mentioned. You can also describe him to the Ai Gift Pro gift finder, which suggests specific ideas with a reason for each.
