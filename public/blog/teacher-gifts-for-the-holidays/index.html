Holiday gifts for teachers that teachers say they want

Published November 28, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

The best holiday gifts for teachers are a gift card to a store they already use, like Amazon, Target or a nearby coffee shop, plus a handwritten note from your child. Teachers ask for this more than anything else. If you want to add an object, pick something used up or used daily, such as good markers, a desk charger or an insulated tumbler, and skip mugs, candles and lotion.

What do teachers actually want for the holidays?

Teachers are unusually consistent about this, and the answer is less romantic than the gift aisle suggests. When Education Week ran an informal LinkedIn poll of roughly 700 people, 79 percent said they would prefer a gift card, and mugs were wanted by about 7 percent. It was not a scientific survey, but it matches what teachers say every single December in every comment section that will have them.

The reason is practical. A teacher with 25 students can receive 25 gifts in the last week before break, and most of them are some version of an apple, a candle, a lotion or a mug that says "Best Teacher." Each one is kind. Collectively they fill a cabinet. A gift card, by contrast, turns into groceries, a dinner out, or the classroom supplies many teachers already buy with their own money.

The second thing teachers mention, nearly as often, is a note. GreatSchools collected teacher comments on holiday gifts, and one put it plainly: "Heartfelt letters mean the most." A few sentences from your child, or from you, about one specific thing the teacher did this fall will outlive anything you buy. Teachers keep these in folders for years and pull them out on bad days.

So the formula that rarely misses is a modest gift card plus a handwritten note. Everything below is for families who want to give a small object as well, or who know the teacher well enough to aim.

Teacher gifts that get used in the classroom

Many teachers spend their own money on supplies, so a gift that replaces one of those purchases is a gift that pays them back. The trick is to buy the specific item they would choose, not a generic "teacher supplies" kit.

For the teacher who makes everything by hand

Elementary teachers laminate constantly: name tags, task cards, anchor charts, anything that has to survive small hands. The school laminator is usually down the hall with a line. A Scotch PRO Thermal Laminator (TL906) appeared on the We Are Teachers list of teacher-recommended gifts for exactly this reason, and it fits on a desk. It is a better pooled class gift than an individual one.

For the teacher who grades with a color system

A pack of Amazon Basics fine-tip markers sounds dull until you remember how fast a classroom goes through them. Pick a big multicolor pack. It will be gone by March, which is exactly the point.

For the teacher whose phone is always at 5 percent

Teachers run on laptops, document cameras and a phone that also handles parent messages. A spare Anker Nano 30W USB-C charger that lives in the classroom drawer means they stop carrying one back and forth. Engadget included it in its teacher gift picks as the spare adapter people actually reach for.

Small luxuries for the teacher who gives all day

If you want something personal rather than practical, aim at the parts of the school day that wear people down: cold coffee, a long walk to the staff room, dry hands from constant sanitizer, and no time for lunch.

  1. A drink that stays cold or hot through fourth period. The Owala 40 oz insulated tumbler was one of NBC Select's teacher picks, and one of its editors wrote that hers "never leaves my desk at work." It is big enough that a teacher can fill it once in the morning.
  2. A coffee that does not go cold during morning duty. The Ember Mug 2 keeps a drink at a set temperature, which matters to someone who pours a cup at 7:40 and drinks it at 10:15. Both NBC Select and Engadget listed it. It sits at the top of most budgets, so it works best as a pooled gift.
  3. Hand sanitizer they will not mind using 40 times a day. Touchland Power Mist is a spray sanitizer that smells good and does not leave hands sticky. It was the first pick on NBC Select's teacher list.
  4. Chocolate that is a step up from the staff room tin. A Tony's Chocolonely sampler or a bag of Lindt Lindor truffles gets eaten, not stored. Check for allergies first if your child has mentioned any.
  5. A better tea break. A Tea Forte Petite Presentation Box looks like a gift without being one more object on a shelf, and it is gone by spring.

Notice what is missing: candles, lotions, scented anything, and decor. Those are the items teachers receive in multiples. If you are buying for several staff members at once, the same logic applies as in our gifts for coworkers under $20 guide: small, consumable, impossible to misread.

How much should you spend on a teacher gift?

Most families spend somewhere in the range of a modest gift card, and teachers consistently say the amount matters far less than the gesture. A $10 card with a real note lands better than a $40 basket with a printed tag. If you want a broader framework for amounts, our guide to how much to spend on a gift walks through it by relationship.

There is also a rule many parents never hear about. Teachers in public schools are public employees, and some states limit what they can accept. In Massachusetts, the State Ethics Commission's guidance on gifts to public school teachers says a public employee generally "may not accept any gift worth $50 or more" given because of their position. The same guidance allows class gifts from students and parents with an aggregated value of up to $150, as long as the gift is identified only as from the class and the teacher is not told who gave what. Gifts to the classroom itself are treated differently from gifts to the teacher.

Other states and individual districts have their own rules, and some are stricter. If the room parent is organizing a pooled gift, it is worth a quick look at the district's policy first. It protects the teacher, not just you.

Is a gift card a good teacher gift, or is it impersonal?

It is the most requested teacher gift there is, so no, it is not impersonal to the person receiving it. The parent buying it may feel that way, which is why the note matters. The card handles the useful part; the note handles the personal part.

Pick a store the teacher actually uses. Amazon and Target are the safest choices because they cover groceries, household items and classroom supplies in one place. A local coffee shop near the school is a nice touch if you have seen the cup on their desk. If you want to mark it as a classroom gift, an Amazon gift card in a greeting card lets your child write directly inside.

A donation is another good route for a teacher who has a project in mind. GreatSchools notes that some teachers would welcome a donation to their classroom through DonorsChoose or a pooled class wish list. Ask the room parent whether the teacher has one.

What not to give a teacher

  • Another mug. About 7 percent of respondents in the Education Week poll wanted one. Most teachers have a cupboard of them.
  • Scented candles and lotions. Teachers get these in multiples, and fragrance is personal.
  • Apple-themed decor. It is charming once. A teacher who has taught for ten years has seen it ten times.
  • Alcohol, unless you know the teacher well and the school allows it. A gift your child carries into a classroom should be something a child can carry into a classroom.
  • Anything expensive from one family. It can put a teacher in an awkward spot, and in some states it runs into the rules above.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best holiday gift for a teacher?

A gift card to a store the teacher uses, such as Amazon, Target or a nearby coffee shop, paired with a handwritten note from your child. In an informal Education Week poll of roughly 700 people, 79 percent said they would prefer a gift card. The note is what teachers say they keep for years.

How much should I spend on a teacher's holiday gift?

There is no set amount, and teachers say the gesture matters more than the price. A small gift card with a sincere note is plenty from one family. Larger amounts work better as a pooled class gift, and some states limit what public school teachers can accept, so check your district's policy.

Can public school teachers accept gifts from parents?

Usually yes, within limits that vary by state and district. In Massachusetts, State Ethics Commission guidance says public employees generally may not accept gifts worth $50 or more given because of their job, while class gifts identified only as from the class may total up to $150. Many districts publish their own rules.

Are homemade gifts good for teachers?

Handmade cards and notes are among the gifts teachers value most, because they are specific to the child and the year. Homemade food is a kind gesture but riskier, since some teachers have allergies or school rules about it. A drawing or letter from your child has no such problem.

Should I give a gift to every teacher, including specials teachers and aides?

It is a kind gesture but not expected. If you want to include art, music or PE teachers, aides and bus drivers, keep it small and consumable, like a chocolate bar or a short note. Recognition matters more to them than the size of the gift.

Is it okay to give a teacher a gift card instead of a present?

Yes. Gift cards are the most requested teacher gift, because they turn into groceries, a meal out or classroom supplies. Choose a store the teacher already uses and add a few handwritten lines so it feels personal.
