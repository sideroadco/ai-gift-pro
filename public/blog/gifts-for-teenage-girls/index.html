Gifts for teenage girls (13 to 17) that fit her real life

Published November 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

The best gifts for teenage girls fit a habit she already has: a Fujifilm Instax Mini 12 for the girl who photographs everything, a Laneige Lip Sleeping Mask or Kitsch heatless curlers for her beauty routine, a Kindle Paperwhite for the reader, or an Owala FreeSip bottle for school. Choose by how she spends her free time, avoid guessed makeup shades and clothes, and use a gift card when her taste is specific.

What do teenage girls actually want as a gift?

Most teenage girls want a gift that fits the life she is already living: her room, her routine, her friends and the small rituals she has built around them. She does not want to be improved. A gift that hints she should read more, journal more or dress differently tends to land as a comment, not a present.

The lists that rank for this search are long, and many of them read like a scroll through a shopping feed. That is useful for ideas and useless for choosing. A 13 year old who still loves plush animals and a 17 year old with a part time job and a driving test next month are both "teen girls," and they want very different things. So start with three questions: where does she spend her free time, what does she carry every day, and what does she already collect?

If you can answer those, you can skip most of the guesswork. If you can only describe her in a sentence or two, type that into our free gift finder and it will give you a dozen ideas with a reason for each. Then use the sections below to narrow it down.

Gifts for her room, her friends and her photos

A teenager's bedroom is the one space she controls, and a lot of the best gifts make it feel more like hers. The second big category is anything that turns time with friends into something she can keep.

An instant camera. The Fujifilm Instax Mini 12 is on the CNN Underscored teen girl gift list for good reason: it has a selfie mirror and handles exposure for her, so the photos come out without fiddling. Physical prints end up on mirrors, in phone cases and on bedroom walls, which is the point. Add a couple of packs of Instax Mini film, because the camera without film is a paperweight on Christmas morning.

A phone photo printer, if she already has thousands of pictures. Some teens would rather print the best shots from their camera roll than take new ones. The Fujifilm Instax Mini Link prints straight from her phone, and Grown and Flown, which builds its teen girl guide with input from parents of teenagers, includes it for exactly that girl.

A blanket she will steal back from everyone. The Barefoot Dreams CozyChic Throw is a splurge, but it is soft enough that it becomes her blanket for years. For a smaller budget, a 14 inch Squishmallow still delights plenty of 13 and 14 year olds, even the ones who claim they are too old for it.

A speaker that can get wet. The Ultimate Ears Wonderboom 4 is waterproof and floats, which means it can live in the bathroom, go to the pool and survive being tossed in a beach bag.

Beauty and hair gifts that are safe bets

Beauty is where many adults panic, because shades, skin types and brand loyalties are personal. The fix is to stay with products that suit almost everyone and leave makeup colors to her.

  1. Lip care. The Laneige Lip Sleeping Mask is an overnight lip treatment that has been a teen favorite for years. It is small, it feels like a treat, and no shade matching is involved.
  2. Heatless curls. A Kitsch heatless curler set gives her waves overnight without a hot tool. Parents like it for the same reason teens do: less heat on her hair and no curling iron left on in the bathroom.
  3. Body cream with a cult following. Sol de Janeiro Brazilian Bum Bum Cream shows up on teen wish lists constantly. A travel size is a good stocking stuffer if the full jar feels like too much.
  4. Mascara she has probably already asked for. Maybelline Lash Sensational Sky High is a drugstore mascara with a big following, and it is one of the few makeup items where shade barely matters.

If she has started wearing makeup but you do not know her shades, a gift card to the store she already shops at beats a guessed foundation every time. Teens are specific about this, and a wrong match goes straight into a drawer.

Practical gifts she will not roll her eyes at

Every parent wants to give something useful. The trick is to pick a useful thing that already has status with teenagers, so it reads as a want, not a chore.

The water bottle everyone at school carries. The Owala FreeSip 24 oz lets her sip through a straw or tip it back, keeps drinks cold through a long school day, and comes in enough colors that she can pick one that feels like hers. Grown and Flown lists it, and so does NBC Select in its teen gift guide, which was put together by asking teens.

A backpack that lasts. The Fjallraven Kanken has been a teen staple for long enough that it no longer counts as a trend. It is durable and water resistant, and the larger sizes hold a laptop. Ask what color she likes, or check what her friends carry and pick something different.

A tracker for the things that keep going missing. If she loses keys or leaves her bag at practice, an Apple AirTag 4 pack finds them through Find My. It works with an iPhone only, so confirm what phone she has first.

Footwear she lives in. Crocs Classic Clogs topped NBC Select's teen list as the best overall pick, partly because she can personalize them with charms. Get her usual shoe size; Crocs run roomy, and most teens already know their Crocs size.

For the reader, the writer and the night owl

Some teenage girls disappear into books, and they are often the easiest to buy for. A Kindle Paperwhite is waterproof, has a glare free screen that reads like paper, and holds hundreds of books, which matters for a kid who finishes a series in a week. Both CNN Underscored and Grown and Flown recommend it. Our guide to gifts for readers has more ideas if she already owns one.

For the teen who writes, a guided journal like The Five Minute Journal gives her a short prompt each morning and night. Give it only if she already likes writing; for a teen who does not, a journal is homework in disguise.

Night owls who watch movies in their room will get a lot out of a Kodak Luma 350 portable projector, which turns a bedroom wall into a screen for sleepovers. It is a bigger gift, so it works well as the one main present rather than one of several.

How much should you spend on a teenage girl?

For a daughter, a common range is one main gift plus a few small ones, and the total depends far more on your family than on any rule. For a niece, a friend's daughter or a babysitter, $25 to $50 is generous and normal. Our guide to how much to spend on a gift walks through the usual ranges by relationship.

The bigger point is that price does not predict whether a teen likes a gift. A $20 lip mask she uses every night beats a $150 gadget she did not ask for. Spend where you know her taste, and save where you are guessing.

What should you avoid buying a teenage girl?

A few gifts miss so reliably that they are worth naming.

  • Clothes you picked on your own. Teen style changes fast and is often specific to her friend group. If you want to give clothing, give a gift card or shop with her.
  • Makeup in guessed shades. Foundation, concealer and bold lip colors are personal. Stick to lip care, mascara and skincare that suits everyone.
  • Anything with a lesson attached. Self help books, chore charts and "you should get into this" hobby kits rarely land.
  • Games and media rated above her age. Some teen lists include titles rated Mature. Check the rating before you buy, especially for a younger teen.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good gift for a 13 year old girl?

A good gift for a 13 year old girl is something playful she can use with friends, like a Fujifilm Instax Mini 12 instant camera, a Squishmallow plush, a waterproof speaker, or heatless curlers. At 13 most girls still enjoy fun, collectible things, so you do not need to buy anything grown up yet.

What do 16 year old girls want for Christmas?

Sixteen year old girls tend to want things that feel more grown up and fit a busier life: a Kindle Paperwhite, a good backpack, skincare she has already mentioned, an insulated water bottle like the Owala FreeSip, or a gift card to the store she shops at. Ask a sibling or friend what she has been talking about.

Is a gift card a good gift for a teenage girl?

Yes. A gift card is one of the most welcome gifts for a teenage girl, especially for clothes and makeup, where her taste is specific. Choose the store she already shops at and pair it with a small physical gift so there is something to open.

How much should you spend on a teenage girl for Christmas?

For a niece, family friend or babysitter, $25 to $50 is a normal and generous range. For your own daughter, families vary widely, and one main gift plus a few small ones is a common pattern. What matters more than the amount is picking something that fits her routine.

What are good stocking stuffers for teenage girls?

Good stocking stuffers for teenage girls include a Laneige Lip Sleeping Mask, a mascara like Maybelline Sky High, a travel size body cream, Instax film, hair clips, and an AirTag. Small things she uses daily beat novelty items that end up in a drawer by January.
